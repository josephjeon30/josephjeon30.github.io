Central limit theorem

Take many samples from any population, average each one, and the averages pile up into a bell curve.

Population: 1,000 numbers between 1 and 200

Latest sample's mean (x̄), with its picked values as dots

Sampling distribution of the mean

Each sample picks 5 numbers from the population (with replacement) and records their average.
Sample means Normal curve the CLT predicts: mean μ, spread σ/√n Latest sample mean

What you're seeing

The top chart is the population. With the default setting it's flat: every number from 1 to 200 is about as likely as any other, so it looks nothing like a bell curve.

Each sample takes n numbers at random from that population and averages them. The bottom chart collects those averages. As samples accumulate, they form a bell shape centred on the population mean μ. The curve narrows as n grows, and its width follows σ/√n, where σ is the population's standard deviation.

Try Two humps or Skewed: the population changes completely, but with n around 30 the averages still form a bell. That's the central limit theorem. Set n = 1 and the "averages" are single values, so the bottom chart just copies the population.
